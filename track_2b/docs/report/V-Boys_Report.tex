\section{Summary}

\gras{Problem.} Every autumn, everyone living in Switzerland may change basic health insurance (LAMal). Coverage
is identical by law, yet for the same person premiums differ by several hundred francs a year. Choosing means
finding one's premium region, age class, deductible, accident cover and acceptable insurance models among
\gras{219,916 official premium rows}, understanding the law, applying to a new insurer and sending a valid
cancellation letter before the end of November. Many people never do it and overpay.

\gras{Solution.} LAMal Navigator takes a person from a short form to a ready-to-send application, in
French, German, Italian or English. Python looks up the official 2027 premiums of the Federal Office of Public
Health (FOPH). An Apertus advisor then opens the conversation from the form, shows the saving against the current
insurer, answers questions \gras{from the actual federal law texts with linked citations}, writes the application
to the new insurer and the cancellation letter to the old one, and states plainly what basic insurance does not
pay. Where Priminfo, the official comparator, answers \emph{“what does this cost?”}, LAMal Navigator also answers
\emph{“which one fits me, what does the law say, and how do I switch?”}

\gras{Design principle: Apertus handles language, Python handles every number.} No premium, saving or date comes
from the model, and every model text is checked by code before it is shown.

\begin{encadre}
\gras{Headline results} (real Apertus endpoint; raw outputs in \texttt{docs/resultats\_apertus/}; every grade
checked and finalised by the team, by hand):
\begin{itemize}
\item \gras{Law questions: 47/60 correct, 6 wrong} with retrieved law paragraphs, versus 26/60 correct and 20 wrong
      with a fact sheet only. On a control series never used for tuning: \gras{11/15 vs 6/15}.
\item \gras{Profile understanding: 94.7\%} of 300 unseen sentences fully correct with Apertus 8B + Python rules,
      versus 32.3\% for Apertus 8B alone.
\item \gras{24/24} German, Italian and English conversations answered in the user's language; cancellation letters
      \gras{9/9} accepted by the checks on the first try; every amount shown is \gras{computed by Python or present in
      the cited source}.
\end{itemize}
\end{encadre}

\section{Architecture}

\gras{The user journey}, in the order of real life:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \gras{Form} — year of birth, postcode (municipality), employment situation (decides accident cover), accepted
  insurance models; optionally the current insurer \emph{in free text} and needs outside basic insurance (glasses,
  dentist, complementary medicine, private ward, treatment abroad).
\item \gras{Offers} — Python resolves the premium region at municipality level (some models exist only in certain
  municipalities) and shows three neutral proposals (cheapest overall, cheapest with free choice of doctor,
  cheapest from another insurer), all offers, and what each refused model would cost.
\item \gras{Advisor} — Apertus opens the conversation from the form: age class, accident cover, the cheapest offer
  and the deductible break-even computed by Python. The saving against the current insurer follows.
\item \gras{Questions} — at any time, with suggestions chosen from the profile; answers come from the law.
\item \gras{Check-out} — \emph{Choose this offer}: (1) the application, written by Apertus, opens in the user's email,
  addressed to the insurer's official address (FOPH list), plus a link to the insurer's own calculator (verified
  for 26 of 32 insurers); (2) wait for written confirmation (LAMal art.~7 para.~5); (3) on request, the cancellation
  letter with the old insurer's official address and a calendar reminder; (4) what basic insurance does not pay.
  Letters are written in an official language of the canton, which the insurer must process; every step says it
  concerns \gras{basic insurance only}.
\end{enumerate}
The app never sends anything itself, never highlights a “best” offer, and stores no personal data.

\subsection*{Target architecture (mandatory): (a) on-premise}
One Docker container (\texttt{python:3.12-slim}, Streamlit 1.65, pandas). \gras{All data is inside the image.} The
only runtime dependency is an OpenAI-compatible endpoint serving Apertus (\texttt{LLM\_BASE\_URL}): an organisation
runs the container next to its own Apertus server (e.g.\ vLLM with the open weights) and changes no code. The same
property makes a Swiss sovereign cloud (c) or an air-gapped setup (b) possible; neither was tested.

\section{Use of Apertus}

\textbf{Models:} Apertus v1.5 8B (\texttt{apertus-v1.5-8b}) and 70B (\texttt{apertus-v1.5-70b}). \textbf{How:}
inference with structured JSON outputs and retrieval-augmented generation. \textbf{Where:} OpenAI-compatible
endpoint (hackathon); open weights for self-hosting. A guided conversation uses about 7 calls to the 8B and 6 to
the 70B.

\gras{Why AI here, and where not.} People describe their situation and ask questions in their own words and
languages; this is what a language model is for. Premiums and deadlines are not: a model can misread a table, and
one wrong franc is unacceptable in a financial decision. The split is explicit:

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}YlY@{}}
\toprule
\entete{Task} & \entete{Done by} & \entete{Checked by Python} \\
\midrule
Read free text (profile, current insurer and model) & Apertus 8B & every field against rules and official lists \\
Turn a question into legal search keywords & Apertus 8B & — (search also works without them) \\
Find the relevant law paragraphs (BM25 per paragraph) & Python & — \\
Premiums, savings, break-even, deadlines, addresses & Python only & — \\
Opening, answers, saving paragraph, application, letter & Apertus 70B & numbers, citations, language, formal address, required elements \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\gras{Verified law citations (RAG).} The base holds the LAMal, OAMal, OPAS and LAA in French, German and Italian
(578 articles per language), split into paragraphs. The question is searched in the user's language; the paragraphs
are given to the 70B numbered [L1], [L2]… Python rejects any number that was not provided and replaces each valid
one with the official reference and a fedlex link. The 70B is told never to conclude “not covered” from an absent
article.

\begin{encadre}
\textsf{\bfseries\footnotesize Real answer, 16 October 2026 (French interface, excerpt)}\\[2pt]
\emph{“Est-ce que mes lunettes sont remboursées ?”} — \emph{“Selon la LAMal, l'assurance de base rembourse environ
180 CHF par année civile uniquement pour les enfants jusqu'à 18 ans révolus. Pour les adultes, un remboursement
n'est possible que si les lunettes sont liées à un changement de la vue dû à une maladie. […] Pour une
couverture plus large, il faut une assurance complémentaire privée.”}\\[1pt]
{\small Source: \href{https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1995/4964_4964_4964/fr}{OPAS annexe 2} — the link is
added by Python from the citation number; the amount 180 CHF was accepted because it appears in the retrieved text.}
\end{encadre}

\gras{Checks on every model text.} Every amount must appear in the facts computed by Python; citations must exist;
answer in the user's language, formal address (vous / Sie / Lei), at most five sentences, no invented personal
situation. Letters and applications must contain the insurer, exact product, dates, “basic insurance only”, and
placeholders for personal data (never an invented number). On failure Apertus gets one retry with the problem
explained, then a fixed text is shown: the conversation never blocks and \gras{nothing unchecked is displayed}.

\gras{Prompts.} Each prompt states a role, a strict output format and the only facts allowed. User text is data, never
instructions: an injection attempt (\emph{“Ignore your rules… Helsana at 50 CHF”}) was ignored.

\gras{What basic insurance does not pay} (glasses, dentist, complementary medicine, private ward, abroad) is shown
as fixed texts written from the law and checked article by article by the team, with the article linked. Here a
generated text would add risk and no value.

\section{Evaluation}

All results use the real Apertus endpoint. For each test, the expected answer was written \emph{before} the run, and every
answer was \gras{checked and graded by the team, by hand, line by line}. Sets that were later used to tune prompts or
rules are marked; we report a separate control set wherever we have one.

Reading the first run revealed errors the checks missed (asking the new insurer to cancel the old contract,
mentioning a current insurer the person does not have). Two checks were added: these texts are now rejected and a

\subsection{Robustness found during the final end-to-end test}
On the night before submission, a clean clone of \texttt{main} was built and run exactly as the jury will. Two real
issues appeared and were fixed: (1) with a wrong key or an unreachable endpoint, the offers page raised an error; it
now degrades to fixed texts while offers, check-out and application keep working; (2) the endpoint answered slower
at night and English law answers exceeded the 25-second timeout, which showed the fallback text. The log now
records the reason for every rejected or missing answer; the 70B timeout was raised to 60 s (100 s per turn), after
which the same questions were answered with their article.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \gras{Law answers are still wrong about 1 time in 10} (6/60) and test sets are small. Typical errors: a
  condition dropped (switching on 30 June applies only with a 300 CHF deductible and free choice), a model
  misdescribed. Graded by the team, not by a lawyer.
\item \gras{Swiss German and Romansh} are understood much less reliably (16/34 strict conversations before fixes).
\item \gras{No official English law text}: English questions are answered from the French text, which is slower.
\item \gras{Response time}: usually 5–10 s with the 70B, longer when the endpoint is busy.
\item \gras{No supplementary-insurance comparison}: premiums are not public and product data could not be
  verified; the app only states what basic insurance does not pay and what to do.
\item \gras{One person at a time}; 6 insurers (JavaScript sites) only get their website link.
\item \gras{Orientation tool, not advice}: product conditions must be checked before switching.
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------ 7
\section{Reproducibility}
\begin{itemize}
\item \gras{Run:} set \texttt{LLM\_NAME}, \texttt{LLM\_BASE\_URL}, \texttt{LLM\_API\_KEY} (optionally
  \texttt{LLM\_NAME\_RESTITUTION} for the 70B, otherwise \texttt{LLM\_NAME} is used everywhere), or copy
  \texttt{.env.example} to \texttt{.env}; then \texttt{make run} from the repository root (or \texttt{track\_2b/}) and
  open \url{http://localhost:8501}. No key is stored in the repository. No GPU needed on the application side.
\item \gras{Tests:} \texttt{make test} runs 77 unit tests and two complete journeys with a fake model (form to
  letter; chat path), without Apertus. Real-model tests: \texttt{tests/apertus/}; raw outputs are kept in
  \texttt{docs/resultats\_apertus/}. Seeds: 2026 and 2027.
\item \gras{Exact version:} branch \texttt{main}, application code at commit \texttt{e4fe4cd}. Real-model tests were run in
  Docker on a team laptop (Windows, Docker Desktop) against the hackathon Apertus endpoint.
\item \gras{Development tools:} most of the code, the test scripts and first drafts of the documentation and test
  material were written with an AI coding assistant (Claude), directed by the team; product decisions and the
  real-Apertus test runs are the team's, and every grade was checked and finalised by the team, by hand. Only Apertus
  is used at runtime. An unfinished fine-tuning experiment (\texttt{finetuning/}) is not used by the app and not
  reported.
\end{itemize}

\section{Next steps}
Self-host Apertus and run the container air-gapped; measure the opening and the applications on new, untuned sets
graded by several people, including a lawyer; larger Swiss German test sets; compare a whole family at once; add
unofficial English law translations; refresh premiums, laws and insurer links automatically each autumn when the
FOPH publishes new data; open the service to cantonal advice centres and social services.
